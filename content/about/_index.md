I'm a high school student who's still dipping her toes in the world out there. The things I write about aren't very specialised for that reason, but all the same I try to go about it with reason and logic. 

This site is meant to store everything from my musings to articles I put effort into writing. None of this will be seen until quite a few years later (or whenever I have the gall to share it), but there is something really nice seeing your work consolidated into a neat, pristine site.

> Knowing your site will survive for decades to come gives you the mental wherewithal to tackle long-term tasks like gathering information for years, and such persistence can be useful if one holds onto every glimmer of genius for years, then even the dullest person may look a bit like a genius himself.  
>  
> -- [Gwern Branwen](https://gwern.net/about#long-content)

But that wasn't the only reason I set about creating this site. I've always had this obsession with designing things[^1]. It started small, with typography (oh I should write about my favourite fonts one day - I'm quite particular). But now I enjoy a general aesthetic (there isn't really a name for it[^2]) that isn't just restricted to fonts. I hope you find this site as aesthetically appealing as I tried to make it be.

It was also fitting that a nice site motivated me to _create_ more. I think it's similar for readers. A nice site motivates them to read more because if the author's put so much heart into creating one, you'd be more inclined to trust their work too. 

Anyway, this is all just the origins of this site. You haven't really learnt about it yet. So here we go. 

## The Content
**Long form blog posts**, which are not necessarily all personal. I try to do a few informational ones here and there. Some can read like a diary. **Short form posts**. An accumulation of what I (would like to, but don't actually) jot down daily, likely to be a more personal. **Pictures** of my life (impersonal ones). **My recent interests**, where I'll update with my progress and also a few posts detailing what I've learnt. E.g., book reviews, math problems that caught my eye. As of right now (5 Nov 2026), I'm interested in _typing_, _debate_, _reading_, _writing_ and _math_. Only a few (e.g., typing) is likely to change in the far future. 

There's also some **meta** stuff, of course. The changelog, design of the site, etc.

## My Experience Writing
I don't think I'm an awful writer? I started writing (long form) when I was going through some issues, and from then on I never stopped. I have significantly less baggage now, but sometimes I've stuck in a bad spot again and writing is always cathartic.

My writing started out very _affected_. I was trying to sound intellectual, maybe? I don't know what came over me, but I recall it because even as the writing is horrible it took a long time to wring out. My thoughts came out in long, winding sentences that jutted out here and there. I'm not proud to say this but I sent them into an LLM hoping they'd polish it up and they did, but then it just got that faint nasty whiff of LLM prose, though not as bad since the LLM did the polishing instead of writing. I did write some pretty good emotional recounts, though, probably because there was so much emotion stirring in me that it had to be dumped on paper somehow. 

Most of my writing is natural. I try not to think about it too much, and it kind of just flows as I go. It's weirdly different from thinking, where I have to actively think and squeeze out the next sentence. (Which is also why I, as of Nov 2026, tried to improve my typing speed, so that whatever _flow_ could be easily transcribed. It's a lot of momentum.) However my writing is only natural when I'm well-versed in that topic, which is conveniently the case for most personal writing, also why I did a lot of that previously. I'm trying to phase away from that and include something informational from time to time, and I think debate is my next best bet. I'm sufficiently emotionally invested in it (having spent 30mins/1hr getting into the correct mindset) that logic and reason comes easily. It took some practice too, but I'm glad I'm here. 

Another thing I've noticed is that the mode of writing matters. I type really eloquently on Whatsapp (yeah, of all places) when I have something to say, but then I sit down before my keyboard and the whole mood shifts. But when I return to my phone the magic's already died out. It's better now, but previously I couldn't for my life reproduce the same quality of texts on laptop. Another is Google Docs and Word. I started out preferring Google Docs, but for some reason the writing didn't feel fluid there. It could be that I didn't type as fast then, but on Word each letter succeeds the other like water gushing out in one steady stream, and after I switched to Word I never looked back. 

If you want advice, here's some: 
1. Jot down your thoughts from time to time, anywhere works 
2. Be well-versed in the content you want to write about - this can be something you're specialised in, something you've observed for a long time, or just something you've read up about extensively
3. Get familiar with some mode of writing. If you like writing on a Moleskin go for it, if you prefer Google Docs great, if you (like me) function best when texting, sustain that for a while until you get familiar with writing then try out other modes of writing
4. Write. There's nothing more to it

## `hugo.yaml` (the config file)

For this example site, I defined permalinks for two sections, `post` and `note`, so that the links to pages under these directories will contain the date info, e.g., `https://xmin.yihui.org/post/2016/02/14/a-plain-markdown-post/`. This is optional, and it is up to your personal taste of URLs.

```yaml
permalinks:
  note: "/note/:year/:month/:day/:slug/"
  post: "/post/:year/:month/:day/:slug/"
```

You can define the menu through `menu.main`, e.g.,

```yaml
menu:
  main:
    - name: Home
      url: ""
      weight: 1
    - name: About
      url: "about/"
      weight: 2
    - name: Categories
      url: "categories/"
      weight: 3
    - name: Tags
      url: "tags/"
      weight: 4
    - name: Subscribe
      url: "index.xml"
```

Alternatively, you can add `menu: main` to the YAML metadata of any of your pages, so that these pages will appear in the menu.

The page footer can be defined in `.Params.footer`, and the text is treated as Markdown, e.g.,

```
params:
  footer: "&copy; [Yihui Xie](https://yihui.org) 2017 -- {Year}"
```

Here `{Year}` means the year in which the site is built (usually the current year).

## Custom layouts

There are two layout files under `layouts/_partials/` that you may want to override: `head_custom.html` and `foot_custom.html`. This is how you inject arbitrary HTML code to the head and foot areas. For example, this site has a file `layouts/_partials/foot_custom.html` to support LaTeX math via KaTeX and center images automatically:

```html
<link rel="stylesheet" href="//cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/katex/dist/katex.min.css">
<script src="//cdn.jsdelivr.net/combine/npm/katex/dist/katex.min.js,npm/katex/dist/contrib/auto-render.min.js,npm/@xiee/utils/js/render-katex.js" defer></script>

<script src="//cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@xiee/utils/js/center-img.min.js" defer></script>
```

You can certainly enable highlight.js for syntax highlighting by yourself through `head_custom.html` and `foot_custom.html` if you want.

If you do not like the default fonts (e.g., `Palatino`), you may provide your own `static/css/fonts.css` under the root directory of your website to override the `fonts.css` in the theme.
