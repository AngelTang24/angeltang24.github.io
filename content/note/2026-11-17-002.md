> People always describe jealousy as this sharp, green, venomous thing. Unfounded, vinegary, mean-spirited. But I’ve found that jealousy, to writers, feels more like fear. Jealousy is the spike in my hear rate when I glimpse news of Athena’s success of Twitter—another book contract, awards nominations, special editions, foreign rights deals. Jealousy is constantly comparing myself to her and coming up short; is panicking that I’m not writing well enough or fast enough, that I am not, and never will be, enough. Jealousy means that even just learning that Athena’s signing a six-figure option deal with Netflix means that I’ll be derailed for days, unable to focus on my own work, mired by shame and self-disgust every time I see one of her books in a bookstore display.  
>  
> -- R.F. Kuang, _Yellowface_

Comparing this with my experiences, and realising how true it feels. Jealousy looks a lot like fear. Fear compels you to strike backhandedly and lash out. Jealousy is the insatiable fuel. And in fear everything looks like a threat, especially their triumphs and achievements.
